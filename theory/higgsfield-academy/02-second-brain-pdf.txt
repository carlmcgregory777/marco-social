GLOSSARY
Essential terms
A raised number marks each term's first use in the main text.
01
LLM
A large language model learns patterns from text during training. It
can help explain a subject, write, plan or code. The Model settings
menu under Auto chooses the language model you talk to. A
separate video model generates the footage.
02
Context
The information the assistant needs about your task: who you are,
what you need, what it should do and the form of the answer. Useful
notes, data, images and references can also provide context when
the tool can read them.
03

The form and level of detail of the answer.
In the example: A list with one audience-relevance line per item.

03
DEVELOP A SCENE
Start with your scene
Describe who is there, where they are and what happens. Bring the moment you want to see.
PROMPT
A bartender closes his empty bar at three in the morning. He switches the lights off one by one, and
when he kills the last light, he walks slowly to the big window and stands there, sad and tired,
looking far into the distance. Outside the window, far away in the dark, hangs the Earth - his bar is
in space. Use the person in my attached photo as the bartender. Before you write the video prompt,
ask me the questions you need answered to make this great. Write the prompt only after I've
answered.
WHO
One bartender, using the attached person as the reference.
WHERE
An empty bar at 3 AM. The final view reveals that it is in space.
WHAT HAPPENS
He switches the lights off, walks to the window and looks out at Earth.
IF IT SKIPS THE QUESTIONS
Ask again for the questions first. Answer them before asking for the video prompt, and add any important
detail the interview missed.
ATTACH THE REFERENCE BEFORE SENDING
In the lesson: + > Attach file > Assets > Upload media. Wait for the thumbnail, then send the idea
with the photo instruction and the interview request.
For your own exercise, use one person and one location. Attach your photo if you want to appear. Your
scene can have a different setting and ending.

REVIEW BEFORE GENERATING
Read the prompt first
Compare the instructions with your idea before asking for a paid take.
ORIGINAL EXCERPT
kills the last light
KEEP THIS INSTRUCTION
switching the lights off one by one
The initial prompt reduced the action to the last light. It already kept the window out of frame during the
approach. The correction restores the light sequence while keeping the ending.
PROMPT
Write only the revised prompt, do not generate yet. Show him switching several lights off one by
one before the slow walk to the window. Keep 15 seconds, 16:9, one continuous shot, normal
gravity, my attached photo as the only bartender, and Earth revealed only at the end.
CHECK THE REVISED ACTION
Read the revised prompt for the person, bar, light sequence, continuous camera move and late Earth
reveal. If a line is unclear or changes your idea, ask for another text revision.

FINAL CHECKLIST
Check your finished work
Use these checks on your research answer and chosen video.
Research I can use
The saved answer helps with my task and fits the audience, timeframe and format I requested.
Supported facts
The facts I plan to use have supporting sources. Where dates matter, they fit the period I asked
about.
Clear action and ending
The main action reads clearly, and the ending delivers the moment I intended.
A recognisable person and place
The character and setting stay coherent. Where I used a photo reference, the person remains
recognisable.
Coherent camera and sound
The camera move is understandable, sound fits the action, and unplanned jumps or noises do not
interrupt the scene.
Chosen take and matching prompt saved
The saved video plays through the ending. Its idea and exact generating prompt are saved with it.
A WORKING DRAFT IS VALID
If a video check still needs work, label the take Draft and record the single next fix. You do not need
another paid take to complete this exercise.
KEEP TOGETHER
Research answer, chosen video, idea and exact generating prompt. Add a short note on what worked and
the next fix, if needed.
