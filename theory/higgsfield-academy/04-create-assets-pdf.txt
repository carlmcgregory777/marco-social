GLOSSARY
Essential terms
A raised number marks each term’s first use in the main text.
01
Prompt following
How closely a model follows your instructions, including the requested objects, counts, positions
and details.
02
Text-to-image
Generating an image from a written description.
03
Image-to-image
Generating a new image using an existing image as input. Whether you can also add text
instructions depends on the model.
04
AI slop
An informal term for low-quality AI-generated content. In this lesson, look for unnatural textures,
repetitive details and objects that do not make visual sense.
05
CGI look
An appearance resembling a computer-generated 3D render. Very smooth surfaces or overly
perfect details can create this impression. It can be a deliberate style rather than a defect.
06
Tonal range
The range of tones from dark to light. Examine how much detail remains visible in the shadows,
midtones and highlights.

GLOSSARY
Essential terms / continued
07
Spatial anchor
A visible object used to describe where something should be. For example: "Seat the character
beside the mug in the middle of the counter."
08
Reverse angle
A view of a scene from the opposite direction. For example, first look into the bar from the
entrance, then look towards the entrance from inside.
09
Depth of field
The range of distances that appear acceptably sharp. A shallow depth of field leaves more of the
foreground or background out of focus.
10
Bokeh
The visual character of out-of-focus blur, especially the shape and softness of blurred points of
light.

CREATE A CHARACTER
From casting to a character
sheet
Use AI Director to develop one character, then prepare a reference that can carry into video.
AI Director is the conversational assistant for Cinema Studio. With the Character Sheet skill, it develops casting options from
a short brief, asks about missing wardrobe details and prepares the selected person as a multi-view sheet.
Where Soul 2.0 fits. In this workflow, Soul 2.0 supplies the casting portraits; Seedream builds the finished sheet. The
lesson's portrait test shows greater variation in the Soul faces. That makes it useful for exploring a cast, rather than settling
immediately on a familiar, idealised face.
PROMPT
Create a three-panel character sheet using the attached image as the identity and outfit reference: full-body front
on the left, full-body back in the middle, and a three-quarter portrait from head to upper chest on the right. Preserve
the person's face, age, build, haircut, black leather jacket, dark ribbed crew-neck top, silver hoop earring and other
visible accessories. Keep the same identity, outfit and proportions across all panels. Complete the unseen lower
outfit with full-length, straight-leg charcoal riding jeans and matte-black leather ankle-height motorcycle boots with
rounded toes and low rubber soles. Match the jeans and boots in both full-body views. Use a seamless pure-white
studio background with soft contact shadows. Remove the original environment and props. Show the full figure
from head to shoes with space around it. Use a neutral stance, arms relaxed at the sides, empty hands and a calm,
closed-mouth expression. The back view faces fully away; the portrait clearly shows the face. Use soft, even,
neutral lighting, low contrast, subtle optical diffusion and natural skin and fabric texture. Preserve the outfit's
colours.
Prepared reference: the small face is covered so it does not compete with the larger portrait. The front and back views still
describe the outfit and proportions. This is a reference-preparation technique, not a change to the character's face.

Soul Cinema creates the base location; the layout should support the action you plan to stage.
The brief places a dark wooden counter on the right, booths on the left and a pool table at the far end,
viewed from the doorway. Starting with an empty room makes the architecture and usable space easier to
judge.
PROMPT
Create an empty American dive bar. Place a dark wooden bar counter on the right, with leather
stools and illuminated bottles behind it. Add a few neon signs, booths along the left wall, and a pool
table at the far end beneath a hanging lamp. Frame the shot from the doorway.
Why this option was selected
Of the four bar generations, the selected image comes closest to the intended mood and layout. The first
alternative felt too grainy; the second felt too empty. The decision is about the scene's needs, not
choosing the most elaborate image.

PROMPT SEEDREAM 5.0 PRO
Write a concrete photographic
brief
Give the model a place, a purpose and a camera setup it can turn into a single image.
01
Name the image and the place
Begin with “An empty photographic view of…” and name the setting. Add two or three concrete elements and their materials: small blue tiles, a clear grout grid and a
deep rear wall. This establishes what must be visible.
02
Explain what the space is for
Describe the action the location needs to support: “ample dry open floor for fifteen dancers” or “central open standing space allowing circular camera movement”.
The purpose tells the model where to leave usable space.
03
Put the camera somewhere specific
Give its position, height, direction and lens: centered at the near threshold, 1.4 m high, frontal view, 24 mm spherical lens. A 0.6 m viewpoint close to foliage creates
a different frame. Choose one coherent setup.
04
Describe where the light comes from
Name the source and direction, then the shadow area if it matters: cool overhead and rear light with a dark near foreground, or overcast daylight filtering from
above. Replace vague “cinematic lighting” with a visible condition.
05
Choose the finish and material detail
Set a short palette, the amount of grain if wanted, and one or two tangible textures: bark and leaf veins, or tile grout. These cues are more useful than piling up
“beautiful”, “ultra-detailed” and “8K”.
06
Close with only relevant exclusions
Use a short final list such as “No people, water, writing or logos.” Set aspect ratio and resolution in the controls. The supplied study uses roughly 60–90 words for
location prompts; treat this as a useful starting length, not a hard limit.

Space for movement

Central clearing; low 0.6 m camera; near foliage framing the view.
PROMPT
An empty photographic view of a dense green woodland clearing: tall straight trunks and layered leaves, overcast daylight filtering from above. Low close
camera at 0.6m looking toward central open standing space, near foliage encircling frame edges and allowing circular camera movement. 24mm spherical
lens, rich natural greens, neutral midtones, mild analog grain, tactile bark and leaf veins. No people, writing or logos.
Try it. Keep the place and purpose fixed, then change only the camera height. Compare the foreground, horizon and usable space. If the frame is wrong, revise that
instruction before adding more adjectives.

Cineimage Skill
Turn a simple idea or a reference image into a cinematic location prompt.
Cineimage develops your brief into clear scene, camera and lighting instructions. It defines architecture,
materials and spatial relationships so Soul Cinema can focus on cinematic light, natural texture and a clean
photographic finish.
