GLOSSARY
Essential terms
A raised number marks each term's first use in the main text.
01
Model
An AI system trained to generate content from your inputs.
Image models include Nano Banana, Seedream and Soul
Cinema. Video models include Kling and Seedance.
02
Prompt
Your written instructions for an image or video. A "lazy
prompt" is a brief request that leaves many scene choices to
the model.
03
SuperComputer
Higgsfield's AI agent, bringing together large language
models (LLMs) such as GPT, Claude and Gemini with image
and video generation tools. The language models handle
conversation and planning; the connected generation tools
create the visual output.
04
Preset
A prepared scene setup. Camera movement, lighting and
sound are already designed; the demonstrated preset uses
your image for the main character.

GLOSSARY
Essential terms / continued
A raised number marks each term's first use in the main text.
05
Aspect ratio
The shape of the frame, expressed as width:height. A 21:9
frame is wider in proportion to its height than a 16:9 frame.
06
Resolution
The pixel dimensions of the image. Aspect ratio tells you its
shape; resolution tells you how many pixels make up that
shape.
07
Credits
The units charged for a generation. Check the quote for your
settings. A completed result still uses credits even if you do
not like it.
08
Failed / NSFW
Failed means a generation did not complete. NSFW ("not
safe for work") indicates a content-safety flag. Technical
errors and intellectual property (IP) checks can also stop a
generation. Credits are usually returned automatically for
these failures or blocks; some models have exceptions.
Contact Support if a refund is missing.

START WITH A SMALL IDEA
Start with a small idea
You can begin using generative AI without a creative or technical background, then build your skills
through practice. These tools create text, images, sound and video from instructions and reference
material.
A small business owner might develop a product ad. A creator might make blog visuals or videos in the
style of user-generated content (UGC). A designer might explore motion graphics or build elements for
visual effects (VFX). These are different uses of the same underlying tools.
Start with a short product clip or a visual for a post. Describe what you want, inspect the result, and revise
the parts that need another pass.
These lessons introduce the tools, help you develop an idea with SuperComputer3, and show how to turn a
brief into clear instructions for your own projects.

STRUCTURE YOUR PROMPT
Structure your prompt2
A short request leaves many scene choices to the model1. Describe the details you care about, then
group them so the scene is easy to follow.
01
Subject
Name the main person or object and any important visual traits.
02
Location
Describe the place, surroundings and time of day.
03
Events
Put the actions in order, including how the scene ends.
04
Camera
Describe what stays in frame, the viewpoint and any camera movement.
05
Light + sound
Specify the light source, its direction and the sounds you want.
Prompt
A cyclist in a yellow jacket rides along a quiet city street at sunrise. She slows down and stops
outside a cafe. Keep the bicycle and rider fully in frame as the camera follows from the side. Use
soft morning light and natural street sounds.
Use these five parts as a starting structure. You can change the order or leave a choice open when it does not matter to
your idea.
The yellow jacket identifies the subject. "Slows down and stops" gives the events their order. "Follows
from the side" describes how the camera moves while the action unfolds.
MAKE THE VISUAL CHOICE SPECIFIC
A word such as "dramatic" suggests a mood. To describe a particular lighting setup, name the source and
direction.
Prompt
Light from a window on the left side of the face, with the other side in shadow.

Distinct roles
The small wedge and the much larger leviathan
have recognisable shapes. The leviathan's glowing
weapon trench is both the source of danger and the
target of the counterattack. The pilot gives the
action a human focus.
Cause and consequence
The larger ship fires, the smaller craft evades, the
pilot triggers a missile launch, and the missile
strikes the trench. The explosion follows an
identifiable hit. Each shot carries the action toward
that outcome.
Camera and visual continuity
Screen direction, ship positions and the missile
chase are specified. Passing clouds and changing
ship size give cues for speed and distance. A
shared palette, light sources and hull materials
connect the shots visually.
Focused constraints
Two craft, one visible pilot and one missile limit the
cast and action. The sound instructions tie engines,
breath and impacts to events in the scene.

FULL PROMPT / SETUP
Build the world
The full prompt follows, with frames from the generated video. Compare the instructions with the
result; some details differ.
The wedge approaches the larger leviathan.
PROMPT / SETUP
A duel of scales inside a burning nebula that behaves like a cloudscape: banks of dusty rose and
salmon with plum shadows, translucent sea-green veils, and a molten red giant filling the eastern sky,
its surface crawling with live convection cells. Everything moves. Cloud streams past the lens, hulls
sweep past the camera and pull away into fog, and distance is read by what grows and what
dissolves in the haze. Two craft only. The wedge: a thirty-meter matte-black faceted attack craft, a
flat arrowhead with four round engine bells clustered across its stern, burning white-blue. The
leviathan: a four-kilometer charcoal capital ship shaped like a manta ray - one smooth arched dorsal
ridge, tapering wingtips, dense plated hull - with a single ventral weapon trench at midship that glows
ember-orange whenever the weapon charges, the one breach in its armor. One pilot flies the wedge:
a Black man, dark flight suit, full helmet with the clear visor raised, face fully readable. Geography:
the duel axis runs west to east; the wedge attacks from the west, the leviathan hangs far east, bow
toward the wedge, silhouetted against the red giant. Cameras stay south of the axis, so the wedge
reads screen-left flying screen-right into depth and the leviathan holds the deep right of frame; the
one exception is Shot 5, flown behind the missile low off its left side. Every exterior camera is itself
flying, and the wedge always moves relative to it - passing it, pulling away from it, coming at it, or
being chased - so speed reads through cloud tearing past the lens and hull size changing inside the
shot. The gap starts at 30 km and the wedge closes to 12 km by the launch. Motion cadence: natural
180-degree shutter, fast passes and streaming cloud carry directional motion blur, holds resolve
sharp.
The time ranges on the following pages are specified in the 15-second prompt.

Impact and payoff
Prompt timing: 11.0-15.0s

PROMPT / SHOT 05
Shot 5 · 11.0-15.0s · chase camera at 84°, flying east twenty meters behind the missile and low off

lower-left corner; the one camera off the south side. First frame: the missile's white-orange motor

full length of the ship, the dorsal ridge splits along its spine in a line of fire, both wings fold upward

bank, one shockwave ring racing outward across the entire cloud deck - and the falling hull
sections burn down through the deck in a storm of embers as the bloom holds to the final frame.

PROMPT / COLOR TREATMENT
Grade: painterly filmic softness - low contrast inside the haze, shadows lifted to plum, wide soft
bloom on the star, the engines and the blast. The red giant is the exterior key: it rims the wedge's
nose and upper facets coral from the east and leaves the rest of its hull dark, while the engines

PROMPT / CONSTRAINTS
POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS Craft in the world: exactly two - the wedge and the leviathan. Crew
visible: one pilot, in Shot 3 only. Missiles fired: one. Beam discharges that fire in the clip: one,
across Shots 1-2. Hard cuts: exactly four, at 3.0s, 5.5s, 9.0s and 11.0s. The wedge's closest

cloud rush, impacts as they occur on screen; inside the cockpit the pilot's breath sits forward over
a muffled engine hum; no music, no score, no bgm, no subtitles.

DEVELOP YOUR SCENE
Develop your scene
You can develop a scene with SuperComputer over several messages. Ask for a written proposal,
discuss the details, then request generation when the plan is ready.
01
Ask for a description or a prompt
Ask for either a plain-language scene description or a draft prompt to review.
Prompt
I want a video of a cyclist in a yellow jacket arriving at a cafe. Describe the scene in plain language
first. Do not generate a video yet.
Already know the scene? "Write the prompt first so I can review it before generation."
02
Discuss and refine the scene
Ask questions and request changes until the proposed scene fits your idea.
Prompt
Make the street quiet and set the scene at sunrise. Follow the cyclist from the side and end when
she stops outside the cafe. Show me the revised prompt before generating.
03
Approve the prompt and generate
Read the revised prompt. Check the settings, output count and quoted cost before requesting the
video.
Prompt
That is the scene I want. Use this approved prompt and the agreed settings to generate one video.
Keep the approved prompt with the result. The same prompt can still produce a different video.
